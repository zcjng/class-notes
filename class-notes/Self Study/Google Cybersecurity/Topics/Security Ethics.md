# ⚖️ Security Ethics

[[Google Cybersecurity|← Back to Course Hub]] · [[2026-10-14|Related day: 2026-10-14]]

Key concepts for using **ethics** to protect organizations and the people they serve.

---

## 🚫 Counterattacks Are Illegal

Deploying a counterattack on a threat actor is illegal because of laws like the **Computer Fraud and Abuse Act of 1986** and the **Cybersecurity Information Sharing Act of 2015**, among others. **You can only defend.**

> [!NOTE] Hacktivists
> If the threat actors are **hacktivists** with a political goal, you can be in real trouble — only the army can attack.

### ICJ guidance on counterattacks

The **International Court of Justice (ICJ)** states a person or group *may* counterattack only if:

- The counterattack will **only affect the party that attacked first**.
- The counterattack is a **direct communication** asking the initial attacker to stop.
- The counterattack does **not escalate** the situation.
- The counterattack **effects can be reversed**.

---

## 🔐 Ethics in Practice

- **Confidentiality** means only authorized users can access specific assets or data. As professional ethics: a high level of respect for privacy to safeguard private assets and data.
- **Privacy protection** means safeguarding personal information from unauthorized use:
    - **PII** — any information used to infer an individual's identity (name, phone number).
    - **SPII** — a specific type of PII under stricter handling guidelines (social security numbers, credit card numbers).
    - To effectively safeguard PII and SPII, security professionals hold an ethical obligation to secure private information, identify vulnerabilities, manage organizational risks, and align security with business goals.

---

## 🧭 Professional Ethical Obligations

**Laws** are rules recognized by a community and enforced by a governing entity. As a security professional you must:

- Remain **unbiased** and conduct your work honestly, responsibly, and with the highest respect for the law.
- Be **transparent and just**, and rely on **evidence**.
- Stay **consistently invested** in your work so you can appropriately and ethically address issues that arise.
- **Stay informed** and strive to advance your skills to contribute to the betterment of the cyber landscape.
