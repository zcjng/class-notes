# Controls, Frameworks, and Compliance Study Guide

## Core Principles
Compliance regulations and frameworks are used to assess an organization’s financial compliance and levels of risk. They ensure overall data safety by covering:
* Confidentiality
* Privacy
* Integrity
* Availability
* Security

*Note: Control failures in these areas can lead to fraud.*

* **Sarbanes-Oxley Act (SOX):** Regulates financial reporting and auditing for public companies to prevent corporate fraud.

## Critical Compliance Standards & Frameworks

### 1. FERC-NERC
* **Scope:** Applies to organizations working with electricity or involved with the U.S. and North American power grid.
* **Requirements:** Organizations must prepare for, mitigate, and report any potential security incidents that could negatively affect the power grid.
* **Legal Obligation:** Must adhere to the Critical Infrastructure Protection (CIP) Reliability Standards defined by the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission.

### 2. FedRAMP®
* **Scope:** A U.S. federal government program.
* **Purpose:** Standardizes security assessment, authorization, monitoring, and handling of cloud services and product offerings.
* **Goal:** Provides consistency across the government sector and third-party cloud providers.

### 3. Center for Internet Security (CIS®)
* **Scope:** A nonprofit organization focused on cybersecurity defense.
* **Purpose:** Provides a set of actionable controls used to safeguard systems and networks against attacks.
* **Incident Response:** Offers explicit controls that security professionals must follow if a security incident occurs.

### 4. General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)
* **Scope:** A European Union (E.U.) general data regulation.
* **Purpose:** Protects the processing of E.U. residents’ data and their right to privacy, both in and out of E.U. territory.
* **Key Rules:** 
  * Organizations must be completely transparent about what data they hold on E.U. citizens and why they hold it. Non-compliance results in heavy fines.
  * If a data breach occurs and an E.U. citizen's data is compromised, the organization has exactly **72 hours** to notify them.
### 5. Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI DSS)
* **Scope:** An international security standard.
* **Purpose:** Ensures that organizations storing, accepting, processing, and transmitting credit card information do so in a secure environment.
* **Goal:** Reductions in credit card fraud.

### 6. The Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA)
* **Scope:** A U.S. federal law established in 1996.
* **Purpose:** Protects patients' private health information from being shared without their consent.
* **Core Governance:** Regulated strictly by three rules:
  1. Privacy
  2. Security
  3. Breach notification
* **Legal Obligation:** Organizations handling Protected Health Information (PHI) must inform patients if a breach occurs. Exposure of PHI can lead to identity theft and insurance fraud.
* **PHI Definition:** Relates to the past, present, or future physical or mental health condition of an individual, including plans of care or payments for care.
* **Complementary Framework:** Security professionals should also know the **Health Information Trust Alliance (HITRUST®)**, an assurance program that directly helps institutions meet strict HIPAA compliance.

### 7. International Organization for Standardization (ISO)
* **Scope:** Establishes cross-border international standards.
* **Purpose:** Covers standards related to technology, manufacturing, and management across different countries.
* **Internal Benefits:** Helps organizations improve their operational processes and procedures regarding staff retention, planning, waste reduction, and services.

### 8. System and Organization Controls (SOC type 1, SOC type 2)
* **Scope:** Standards developed by the **American Institute of Certified Public Accountants® (AICPA)** auditing standards board.
* **Focus Areas:** SOC 1 and SOC 2 consist of a series of reports evaluating user access policies at different organizational levels, including:
  * Associate
  * Supervisor
  * Manager
  * Executive
  * Vendor
  * Others
* **Application:** Used to evaluate an organization’s financial compliance and vulnerability to risk. They directly cover data confidentiality, privacy, integrity, availability, security, and data safety to prevent corporate fraud.

This video explains the primary purposes and functions of commonly used security tools that an entry-level security analyst would use.

Understanding Logs

• Definition: Logs are records of events within an organization's systems, such as employee sign-ins or web service access.
• Importance: They are crucial for security professionals to identify vulnerabilities and potential security breaches.

Security Information and Event Management (SIEM) Tools

• Function: SIEM tools collect and analyze log data in real-time to monitor critical activities and identify potential breaches as they happen.
• Examples:
	• Splunk Enterprise: A self-hosted data analysis platform.
	• Google's Chronicle: A cloud-native SIEM tool.

Other Key Security Tools


Playbooks

• Definition: These are manuals that provide detailed instructions for operational actions, such as responding to security incidents.

chain of custody playbook. Chain of custody is the process of documenting evidence possession and control during an incident lifecycle. As a security analyst involved in a forensic analysis, you will work with the computer data that was breached. You and the forensic team will also need to document who, what, where, and why you have the collected evidence. The evidence is your responsibility while it is in your possession. Evidence must be kept safe and tracked. Every time evidence is moved, it should be reported. This allows all parties involved to know exactly where the evidence is at all times.

protecting and preserving evidence playbook. Protecting and preserving evidence is the process of properly working with fragile and volatile digital evidence. As a security analyst, understanding what fragile and volatile digital evidence is, along with why there is a procedure, is critical. As you follow this playbook, you will consult the order of volatility, which is a sequence outlining the order of data that must be preserved from first to last. It prioritizes volatile data, which is data that may be lost if the device in question powers off, regardless of the reason. While conducting an investigation, improper management of digital evidence can compromise and alter that evidence. When evidence is improperly managed during an investigation, it can no longer be used. For this reason, the first priority in any investigation is to properly preserve the data. You can preserve the data by making copies and conducting your investigation using those copies.

Network Protocol Analyzers (Packet Sniffers)

• Definition: Tools that capture and analyze data traffic within a network.
• Examples: tcpdump and Wireshark

Web vulnerability
A web vulnerability is a unique flaw in a web application that a threat actor could exploit by using malicious code or behavior, to allow unauthorized access, data theft, and malware deployment.

To stay up-to-date on the most critical risks to web applications, review the Open Web Application Security Project (OWASP) Top 10.

Antivirus software
Antivirus software is a software program used to prevent, detect, and eliminate malware and viruses. It is also called anti-malware. Depending on the type of antivirus software, it can scan the memory of a device to find patterns that indicate the presence of malware. 

Intrusion detection system 
An intrusion detection system (IDS) is an application that monitors system activity and alerts on possible intrusions. The system scans and analyzes network packets, which carry small amounts of data through a network. The small amount of data makes the detection process easier for an IDS to identify potential threats to sensitive data. Other occurrences an IDS might detect can include theft and unauthorized access.

Encryption
Encryption makes data unreadable and difficult to decode for an unauthorized user; its main goal is to ensure confidentiality of private data. Encryption is the process of converting data from a readable format to a cryptographically encoded format. Cryptographic encoding means converting plaintext into secure ciphertext. Plaintext is unencrypted information and secure ciphertext is the result of encryption.  

Note: Encoding and encryption serve different purposes. Encoding uses a public conversion algorithm to enable systems that use different data representations to share information.    

Penetration testing 
Penetration testing, also called pen testing, is the act of participating in a simulated attack that helps identify vulnerabilities in systems, networks, websites, applications, and processes. It is a thorough risk assessment that can evaluate and identify external and internal threats as well as weaknesses.
