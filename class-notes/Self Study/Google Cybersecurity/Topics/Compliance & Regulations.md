# 📜 Compliance & Regulations

[[Google Cybersecurity|← Back to Course Hub]] · [[2026-10-14|Related day: 2026-10-14]]

Compliance regulations and frameworks are used to assess an organization's financial compliance and levels of risk. They ensure overall data safety by covering **confidentiality, privacy, integrity, availability, and security**.

> [!WARNING] Control failures
> Control failures in these areas can lead to **fraud**.

---

## 📚 Recommended Research

- **Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act (GLBA)** — focuses on financial compliance and protecting consumer financial data.
- **Sarbanes-Oxley Act (SOX)** — regulates financial reporting and auditing for public companies to prevent corporate fraud.

## 🏛️ Key Standards & Frameworks

### FERC-NERC
- **Scope:** organizations working with electricity or involved with the U.S. and North American power grid.
- **Requirements:** prepare for, mitigate, and report potential security incidents that could negatively affect the power grid.
- **Legal obligation:** adhere to the **Critical Infrastructure Protection (CIP) Reliability Standards** defined by the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission.

### FedRAMP®
- **Scope:** a U.S. federal government program.
- **Purpose:** standardizes security assessment, authorization, monitoring, and handling of cloud services and product offerings.
- **Goal:** consistency across the government sector and third-party cloud providers.

### Center for Internet Security (CIS®)
- **Scope:** a nonprofit organization focused on cybersecurity defense.
- **Purpose:** provides a set of **actionable controls** to safeguard systems and networks against attacks.
- **Incident response:** offers explicit controls that security professionals must follow if a security incident occurs.

### General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)
- **Scope:** an European Union (E.U.) general data regulation.
- **Purpose:** protects the processing of E.U. residents' data and their right to privacy, both in and out of E.U. territory.
- **Key rules:**
    - Organizations must be completely **transparent** about what data they hold on E.U. citizens and why; non-compliance results in heavy fines.
    - If a data breach occurs and an E.U. citizen's data is compromised, the organization has exactly **72 hours** to notify them.

### Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI DSS)
- **Scope:** an international security standard.
- **Purpose:** ensures that organizations storing, accepting, processing, and transmitting credit card information do so in a secure environment.
- **Goal:** reductions in credit card fraud.

### Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA)
- **Scope:** a U.S. federal law established in **1996**.
- **Purpose:** protects patients' private health information from being shared without their consent.
- **Core governance** — regulated strictly by three rules:
    1. Privacy
    2. Security
    3. Breach notification
- **Legal obligation:** organizations handling **Protected Health Information (PHI)** must inform patients if a breach occurs. Exposure of PHI can lead to identity theft and insurance fraud.
- **PHI definition:** relates to the past, present, or future physical or mental health condition of an individual, including plans of care or payments for care.
- **Complementary framework:** the **Health Information Trust Alliance (HITRUST®)** is an assurance program that helps institutions meet strict HIPAA compliance.

### International Organization for Standardization (ISO)
- **Scope:** establishes cross-border international standards covering technology, manufacturing, and management.
- **Internal benefits:** helps organizations improve operational processes regarding staff retention, planning, waste reduction, and services.

### System and Organization Controls (SOC 1 & SOC 2)
- **Scope:** standards developed by the **American Institute of Certified Public Accountants® (AICPA)** auditing standards board.
- **Focus:** a series of reports evaluating user access policies at different organizational levels (associate, supervisor, manager, executive, vendor, and others).
- **Application:** used to evaluate an organization's financial compliance and vulnerability to risk; directly cover data confidentiality, privacy, integrity, availability, security, and data safety to prevent corporate fraud.
