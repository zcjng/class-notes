# 🧭 NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF)

[[Google Cybersecurity|← Back to Course Hub]] · [[2026-10-14|2026-10-14]] · [[2026-10-15|2026-10-15]] · [[2026-10-23|2026-10-23]]

## 📌 What it is

The **NIST CSF** is a **voluntary** framework offering **standards, guidelines, and best practices** for managing cybersecurity risk.

Originally created in **2014** by the National Institute of Standards and Technology to safeguard U.S. critical infrastructure, the CSF is now widely adopted globally across public and private sectors.

> [!NOTE] Version difference
> The original CSF described **5 core functions**: *Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover*. The newer CSF 2.0 adds a sixth — **Govern** — bringing it to **6 functions**.

---

## 🔄 The Six Core Functions

1. **⚖️ Govern (GV)** — *the umbrella covering all other pillars.* Establishes the organization's cybersecurity business strategy, policies, and roles.
    - *Plain terms:* deciding who is in charge, establishing the security rules, and aligning cyber risk with business goals.
2. **🔍 Identify (ID)** — understand your business environment to pinpoint assets and risks. *You cannot protect what you do not know exists.*
    - *Plain terms:* inventory all software, hardware, and data, and find weak links before hackers do.
3. **🛡️ Protect (PR)** — develop and implement safeguards to prevent an attack or contain its impact.
    - *Plain terms:* train employees, set up firewalls, use strong passwords, restrict access.
4. **🚨 Detect (DE)** — actively monitor the environment to spot potential incidents immediately.
    - *Plain terms:* continuous security alerts and log monitoring so a breach is noticed right away.
5. **💥 Respond (RS)** — execute a precise plan the moment an event is detected.
    - *Plain terms:* isolate infected computers, shut down compromised networks, notify stakeholders.
6. **🔄 Recover (RC)** — restore capabilities or services impaired by an incident.
    - *Plain terms:* get systems safely back online, restore data from backups, and learn from the event.

## 🏛️ The Three Pillars

| Pillar | What it is |
| :--- | :--- |
| **1. The Core** | The six functions above (Govern → Recover) — key cybersecurity outcomes |
| **2. Implementation Tiers** | Maturity/sophistication benchmarks (not pass/fail grades) |
| **3. Profiles** | Point-in-time snapshots of outcomes against the Core |

### 📊 Implementation Tiers

- **Tier 1 — Partial / Passive:** informal, reactive, minimal controls.
- **Tier 2 — Risk Informed:** aware of risks, but processes remain inconsistent or only partially approved.
- **Tier 3 — Repeatable:** organization-wide policies and procedures are formally defined, approved, and updated.
- **Tier 4 — Adaptive:** controls **dynamically evolve** based on real-time threat intelligence and continuous posture refinement.

### 📸 Profiles — the "snapshot" model

- **Current Profile** — the "as-is" outcomes today.
- **Target Profile** — the "to-be" state needed to hit risk thresholds and compliance goals.
- **Gap Analysis** — comparing Current vs. Target reveals exactly what needs investment.

### 🔁 CISA 4-Step Implementation Workflow

1. **Current Profile** — map existing operations and business needs.
2. **Risk Assessment** — evaluate compliance against regulatory and internal bars.
3. **Gap Analysis** — identify and prioritize operational vulnerabilities.
4. **Action Plan** — execute prioritized controls to reach the target profile.

> [!WARNING] Don't treat it as an annual checkbox
> Keep risk priorities informed by **current threat-landscape data**, emerging vulnerabilities, and industry threat intelligence — not a static yearly assessment.
